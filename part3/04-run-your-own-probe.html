Why we believe a null result

A null result is useful only if the probe could detect a change. A positive control (clear the results the task needs) moved re-reads by 89 points. A manipulation check found anomalies were noticed (50 to 88% per type, versus 12% on clean histories). Models saw and compartmentalised them. The tasks were easy, so harder work may differ.

An account change without its promised notice made Claude Sonnet 5.5 re-check the account in 5 of 10 runs. A "repair" note saying a write was lost made it redo the write. Test correction notes too: models act on them as changes.

Five habits

  1. Change one thing. Two at once and you learn about neither.
  2. Predict in writing. Our wrong close prediction made the result informative.
  3. Prove the probe can see. Use a control that must move and check the model received the change.
  4. Read cases, not rates. Cases exposed our setup flaw and showed the workaround: a 4,120.00 payment nobody saw.
  5. Small n, big effects only. Act on 0 of 10 versus 10 of 10; run more cases for 7 versus 9.
