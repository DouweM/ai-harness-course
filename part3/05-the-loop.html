The loop

The three parts are one loop. Design from what the model is missing, build one idea for it, measure it against what you have on your own cases, and let the misses become the next idea.

One loop, three steps

Every dot is a chapter you've read; the filled one is this one. Tap a part to send Tok there.

Each step is weak alone. An idea you can't try is an opinion; a change nobody measured is a demo; an eval with no idea behind it says a number moved, not why. Most laps improve an agent you already run, and the winner is often a combination of ideas. Starting from scratch is just the first lap, where you try a few directions to see where the good region is.

Close the course on the loop: Part 1 gives you ideas, Part 2 makes trying one cheap, Part 3 says whether it beat what you had, and by how much. The misses are the next idea.

Two laps, from this course

  1. Design. A compaction summary dropped a rule the billing team had given ("never contact a debtor before 9am their time"), and every model broke it, 80 of 80 (Part 1, chapter 4). Idea: tell the model the summary may be incomplete.
  2. Build. One sentence appended to the summary, with a search over the full history available.
  3. Measure. GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash went back to the full history and kept the rule, 39 of 40 across two wordings; Claude Sonnet and Haiku 5.5, 0 of 40. Half a fix.
  4. Next lap. Don't rely on the model to notice: write the summary so it keeps the user's rules, verbatim. Built as a summary_prompt on TieredCompaction (Part 2, chapter 4), added next to billing_world in chapter 6. Measured on the same four models, 10 runs each: every one kept the rule, 40 of 40.

The second lap is the one that shipped. The first was still worth running: it showed which model families can be trusted to notice a gap and which can't, and that changed what the second lap built.

A dropped rule, half a fix, a split by family, then a fix on every model.

A lap on your own agent

One lap on an agent you already run takes a day:

  1. Pick one decision someone corrects today, and export 20 or more corrections.
  2. Write the evaluator for the mistake that costs you.
  3. Run today's version as arm A and read every miss.
  4. Have one idea from what the misses share: what can't it see, whose voice is it, what moved?
  5. Implement it as one change, ideally one capability, and run it as arm B.
  6. Compare, and write down how much better B is on which cases, what you'd ship, what you'd watch online, and the next lap.

You need the agent's code, read access to its traces or corrections, and a model API key. Your coding agent can do the typing; "Ask your agent" below hands it this chapter.

One lap on an agent they already run. Ask the room which decision of their agent gets corrected most.
