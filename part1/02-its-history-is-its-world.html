Part 1 · How the model sees the world

Its history is its world

An agent believes its history completely. When the history is slightly wrong, the agent is confidently wrong. Keep the story true: add to it, never quietly change it.

Monday 23 Nov, 09:20On Wednesday Dana corrected herself: half on Friday, the rest by the 30th. On Friday she wired the first half of INV-2041, 2,060.00. Your agent's history still holds Thursday's lookup: "open, 4,120.00". And on Thursday morning month-end close took mark_paid off the agent's tool list until Monday. Neither change is in any message.

From assistant to agent

Give the assistant from chapter 1 tools and a job, and it becomes an agent: it looks things up, acts, and reads the results, request after request. Every one of those calls and results stays on its page. That history is the agent's picture of the world, and nothing else is.

Its history is the only world it has.

Why

The page is self-consistent: mark_paid worked, and nobody said it stopped. To notice, Tok would have to infer a change from an absence, from a list that's merely shorter than one it read many requests ago. When the world changes and the page doesn't say so, Tok acts on the old world with full confidence. Telling it costs one line.

Edit the past, delete it, or add to it?

Monday, Dana writes: "did you get our first payment?". Thursday's lookup in the history still says "open". Your harness can leave it, edit it to match, append a dated note, delete it to save tokens, or swap it for a placeholder. Each choice gives Tok a different past, and Tok will believe whichever one you give it. Try them.

A hole it has to fill

When the picture has a hole and the answer's required fields leave no room for "I don't know", the agent fills it with the most likely thing. Asked for a required due_date for "this Friday", with nothing on the page saying what today is:

due_date required, no date on the pagewhat came back

Our run, 15 Nov 2026. 5 runs per model. With the date optional, every model left it empty (15 of 15); with the arrival date on the page, every model wrote 20 November (15 of 15). Invented dates are the dangerous kind: they look exactly like real answers.

The same goes for doubt. Given a list to write its doubts in, models sent a contradictory email to a person 30 times out of 30; given a confidence score to fill instead, 0 of 30. A model writes 95 on almost anything, but it will name what bothers it if the answer has a place for that.

Our run, 15 Nov 2026. A promise for Friday plus a line claiming the invoice was settled last week, 10 runs per model per arm on Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash. Every doubts list named the contradiction; GPT and Gemini scored the email 95 to 100, the same as a clean promise. On clean promises no model invented a doubt (0 of 15).

Keep the story true

The point

Tok's history is its world, and it trusts that world completely. When the world and the history disagree, the agent acts on the history, with full confidence. So add to the story instead of changing it: a dated note when a fact goes stale, a stub where you remove a result, one line when a tool leaves, and room in the answer for "I don't know".
