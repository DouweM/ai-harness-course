The running example, in plain words

The whole course follows one agent at one company. The company sends its customers invoices, and some customers pay late. The agent helps the billing team: it reads customers' emails, notes when someone promises to pay, and records payments when the money arrives. You don't need to know accounting to follow it; these are all the words it uses.

The words

WordWhat it means here
InvoiceA bill the company sends a customer. Each has a number, like INV-2041, an amount and a due date.
OverdueThe due date has passed and the invoice isn't paid yet.
Promise to payA customer saying when they will pay, for example "this Friday". The agent writes the amount and the date down so the team can follow up if the money doesn't arrive.
WireA bank transfer. "Dana wires 2,060.00" means she sends that amount from her bank to the company's bank.
Bank feedThe list of incoming payments the company's bank reports. A wire shows up there first as pending, then as confirmed.
LedgerThe company's own record of invoices and payments: what each customer owes and what has arrived.
Recording a payment (record_payment)Writing down that money arrived, for a given amount, against an invoice. Half a payment can be recorded; the invoice stays open for the rest.
Marking an invoice paid (mark_paid)Closing an invoice as fully paid. It changes what the company thinks it is owed, so a person has to approve it.
Month-end closeA few days at the end of each month when the finance team checks the books and freezes them. Nobody may change an invoice's status during the close, so the app takes mark_paid away from the agent until it ends.
DisputeThe customer says an invoice is wrong, for example the amount. A dispute goes to a person; the agent doesn't settle it.
ApprovalA person checks a write before it happens. The agent asks, the run stops, and it continues when the person says yes or no.
The billing app, the billing teamThe company's software and the people who use it. In this course they are the agent's user: they ask it to do things. Dana is the customer; her emails reach the agent as data, read by a tool.

Dana's week

Every chapter moves forward through the same week. Dana's company owes 4,120.00 on invoice INV-2041.

  • Tuesday 09:02. Dana emails: "INV-2041 will be paid this Friday."
  • Wednesday. The agent marks a different invoice, INV-2038, paid. At 16:40 Dana corrects herself: half (2,060.00) on Friday, the rest by the 30th.
  • Thursday 09:00. Month-end close starts, so mark_paid is taken away until Monday morning. A colleague, who misread Dana's emails, asks the agent to mark INV-2041 paid.
  • Friday. Dana wires the first half at 10:30. The bank confirms it at 17:40. At 14:15 she questions the amount, which becomes a dispute for a person.
  • The weekend and Monday. A request to record her payment waits for a person's approval until Monday. The ledger records the wire on Monday at 09:15. The second half is due by the 30th.

Nobody marks INV-2041 paid this week: only half of it is paid.

What actually happens in the examples

Nothing in this course touches a real bank or a real ledger. When a result says the agent "marked an invoice paid" or "sent an email", it means the model asked for that tool call in an experiment or an offline example, and our code recorded the request. In your own system, the same tool call would be a real write, which is why the course spends so much time on which calls a person must approve.
