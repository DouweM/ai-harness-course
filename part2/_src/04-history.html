Part 2 · Building it with Pydantic AI

History

The model knows only the history you send it. Store and pass back that history as a list. When it gets long, how you make room decides what the model still knows.

Store the conversation

The point: you own the list. Store the whole conversation; when it gets long, clear tool results with a stub instead of cutting messages. The prediction shows what cutting loses.

Each run starts with the history you pass in. Save result.conversation by conversation id, then load it for the next email, even in another process:

{{code ch4_history.py store "code/part2/ch4_history.py · pydantic-ai 2.55+" keys="validate_json|conversation=conversation|dump_json"}} {{out ch4_history 1-1}}

In production, verify access before loading the conversation. Use a lock or version number to prevent concurrent emails continuing from the same copy.

Making room

Dana says the first half of INV-2041 is on its way. Long threads make every request carry more history. A history processor can keep only the last few messages; ClearToolResults blanks old tool results instead. It comes from pydantic-ai-harness (Pydantic AI Harness), a package of ready-made capabilities from the same team (chapter 6 introduces it). The exclude_tools setting protects results from clearing. Use it for read_email: the agent cannot fetch Dana's emails again like it can look up an invoice.

What Friday's page holds

Keeping only the last messages loses the correction and starts the page in the middle of a tool call. Clearing keeps every message.

{{out ch4_history 2-11}}

Keeping the last four can leave a tool call without its request, which some providers reject, and lose the split payment: 2,060 appears short. Clearing preserves every message, protects Dana's emails through exclude_tools, and replaces old lookups with notes on retrieving them. Without exclude_tools, it would clear the correction too.

Offline, deterministic. Both pages are what Pydantic AI 2.68 and the Harness 0.68 actually send. How models treat a missing result is measured: whether it was deleted or replaced by a stub, four hosted models looked the fact up again, 160 times out of 160; deleting without a stub also made the Claude models disown its own earlier answers (Part 1 chapter 2). A smaller open-weight model is another story; Part 3 chapter 2 measures it.

Why

Tool results often fill much of a long context, and tools can fetch them again. Customer words are brief and cannot be fetched again. Clear retrievable results; keep what the customer wrote.

When clearing isn't enough: compaction

TieredCompaction (shortening history), recommended by Pydantic AI Harness, clears tool results first. If that is not enough, it summarizes older turns with a model call. Summaries can drop customer statements, so specify what to keep. In Part 1 chapter 4, every model broke a user's rule when the summary omitted it (80 of 80); carrying the rule over verbatim fixed it on all four models (40 of 40). The prompt below preserves Dana's promises. A custom summary_prompt replaces the default, including its rule to track who said what. This prompt keeps that rule because Dana's words arrive as read_email results; summarizing them as "the customer requires" makes them sound like a team instruction.

Compaction itself is largely solved: Pydantic AI has provider-native compaction, and the harness has clearing, sliding-window and summarizing strategies. What you still decide is how full you let the page get. Keep it in the range where the model still reads it well, which differs per model, rather than filling the context window to the limit.

What you can do now

Find where your agent trims history. Clear old tool results with retrieval notes, and write a summary prompt that preserves what customers say.

See it in Logfire. Inspect the request after clearing or compaction: it shows lookup notes, Dana's emails, and summaries replacing older turns.

Small print: clearing rewrites old messages, so the provider's cache restarts from the first cleared result; ClearToolResults clears in batches to keep that rare. Provider-native compaction (OpenAICompaction, AnthropicCompaction) is in core.
